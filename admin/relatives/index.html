    Relatives and Caregivers

    Patients can look after relatives from the patient application: a parent books appointments for their children, a grown-up child helps an elderly parent, two parents share the follow-up of the same child.

    Menu: Settings > Configuration > Patient Management

    The feature is off by default. Enable it with ENABLE_PATIENT_RELATIVES (see Advanced Options). It has no effect while FORCE_TEMPORARY_PATIENTS is on: temporary-only mode forbids the lasting patient accounts a family relies on.

    What patients can do

    Once enabled, My relatives appears in the patient's profile menu.

    Action What happens
    Add a relative Creates the profile of someone who has no account of their own — typically a child: first name, last name, date of birth, gender, and the patient's relationship to them. Nobody signs in as that profile: the people looking after it act for it.
    Book an appointment The booking wizard asks Who is this appointment for? on its review step, whatever the way in — dashboard search, practitioner map, practitioner sheet or the relatives page. The request is made for the relative; the patient remains the one who booked it.
    Invite a caregiver Sends an invitation, by email or text message, to look after the patient themselves or one of the relatives without an account of their own they look after — the other parent of a child, for instance. A relative with their own account chooses their caregivers themselves. An account is created for the invitee when they have none. Nothing is shared before they accept.
    Remove A caregiver stops looking after a relative, or a patient takes back the access they gave. The access ends at once: the follow-ups, the requests made for the relative and their upcoming calls. A profile without access of its own whose last caregiver leaves is closed (deactivated); its follow-ups stay in the practitioners' records.

    A relative can have several caregivers, and a caregiver can look after several relatives. Each link has its own relationship (parent, legal guardian, spouse or partner, child, brother or sister, other relative).

    What a caregiver gets

    A caregiver reads what the patient reads, and nothing more, for as long as the link exists:

    • the follow-ups of the patient, as long as they are visible by the patient — a follow-up hidden from the patient stays hidden from their caregivers;
    • the chat of those follow-ups, and its notifications;
    • the requests made for the patient, whichever caregiver made them. A follow-up booked for a relative belongs to the relative: the caregiver who booked it does not keep it after leaving.

    For a relative without access of their own (no email, no phone number):

    • the caregivers are put on the roster of the appointments of the relative's own follow-ups — when a caregiver books, or when a practitioner schedules one — and receive the invitations and reminders. Their roster rows do not open the follow-up by themselves: the link does;
    • a child added to someone else's appointment (a parent's own follow-up, a standalone call) brings nobody along: the organiser invites who they need;
    • a caregiver who joins later is added to the relative's upcoming appointments;
    • a practitioner calling the relative rings every caregiver; the ringing stops for the others as soon as one picks up;
    • messages addressed to the relative's profile (follow-up assigned, reminders) are delivered to the caregivers instead; nothing is sent to the profile, and no manual access link can be generated for it.

    A relative with their own account keeps receiving their own invitations and calls, manages their own profile, and chooses who looks after them: their caregivers neither see nor withdraw the invitations they send.

    What practitioners see

    On the patient record, Relatives looking after this patient lists the caregivers with their relationship, email address and phone number, for any practitioner allowed to see the patient: they are who to reach about them. A patient without contact details of their own is flagged Looked after by relatives.

    Caregivers show up as regular participants of the appointments they are on.

    Managing links

    Menu: Users & Authentication > Relatives

    Every link is listed with its patient, caregiver, relationship, who sent the invitation and when it was accepted. A link with no acceptance date is an invitation still waiting for an answer: it grants nothing.

    Messaging template

    The invitation uses the caregiver_invitation template, which can be overridden like any other (see Messaging Providers). Its link opens the invitation in the patient application, after sign-in.

    A patient can send up to 20 invitations and add up to 20 relatives a day, with at most 10 invitations waiting for an answer at a time. An account created for an invitation is removed when the invitation is withdrawn before anyone used it.

    Limitations

    • With end-to-end encryption, the follow-up key is wrapped for the caregivers when a patient booking creates the follow-up. The practitioner application does not wrap it for caregivers: on a follow-up a practitioner created, or for a caregiver who joins afterwards, the caregivers can open the follow-up but cannot decrypt its messages.
    • Relatives are not exposed through the FHIR API. An appointment roster written through FHIR keeps the caregivers attending for a child it lists.
